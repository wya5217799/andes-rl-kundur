## Conclusions

- **Registered formulation result - negative**: the sealed SLSQP-based formulation-as-executed cannot complete the registered development bank within its fixed solver contract. Q-0078 therefore closes negative for this exact formulation.
- **Failure boundary**: the observer is constructed and the execution is reproducible; the blocking failure is repeated constrained-solver termination during causal development trajectories. The evidence does not establish that the underlying convex finite-horizon problem is mathematically infeasible.
- **Comparison boundary**: because both arms contain solver failures and are invalid, R325 supplies no retained-cross value, decoupling efficacy, controller superiority, or directional performance comparison.
- **Repair boundary**: a separately prospective solver-adequacy question may compare a specialized convex-QP implementation with the successful SLSQP prefixes and the unchanged development contract. Scratch timing or solver experiments are engineering diagnostics only and are not R325 evidence.
- **Programme boundary**: R325 does not authorize ANDES execution, a physical closed loop, distributed agents, reward definition, or training. The conference title remains exactly unchanged but receives no result support from R325.

## Limits

- Evidence covers only one sealed centralized, model-only controller formulation on two fixed reduced-model points and the registered development bank.
- Solver termination under one algorithm, initialization, tolerance, and budget is not a proof of mathematical infeasibility, closed-loop instability, actuator inadequacy, or impossibility of constrained control.
- Prefix constraint checks do not establish full-trajectory feasibility or safety for failed cases.
- No fresh holdout, physical simulator, nonlinear plant, topology change, communication graph, distributed execution, agent, learning algorithm, EMT, HIL, hardware, or deployment condition was tested.
