## Conclusions

- **Development repair - passed**: one fixed permitted-signal disturbance-aware estimator changes the unchanged retained controller from severe development amplification to passing every frozen absolute development gate.
- **Mechanism boundary**: R328 already isolated the estimation layer. R329 validates the complete augmented-estimator package, but it changes disturbance representation, covariance model, and gain together and therefore does not identify which internal estimator feature contributes how much.
- **Selection boundary**: scratch development outcomes selected this single candidate before the formal seal. R329 establishes deterministic reproducibility and development admission, not an unbiased estimator-performance estimate. The protected fresh holdout is the next required confirmation.
- **Heterogeneity boundary**: the aggregate state-error reduction is not casewise dominance; four cases have higher estimation error despite all 32 cases having better controlled output than the failed parent. The safe mechanism statement is package-level development repair, not universal estimator-accuracy improvement.
- **Stability boundary**: both linear estimation error maps are asymptotically stable under the frozen nominal model, but their radii are close to one. This is not a noise, mismatch, transient-robustness, or physical-stability certificate.
- **Information boundary**: the estimator runtime uses only delayed delivered outputs, executed actions, memory, and fixed model matrices. True state and disturbance appear only in the audit scorer and do not enter estimator or controller calls.
- **Programme boundary**: a separately sealed fresh holdout using the already registered separated-doublet cases and output-mismatch modes is the only next eligible evidence step. Physical execution, distributed agents, reward, training, and diagnostic EVAL remain blocked.

## Limits

- Evidence covers one centralized retained reduced-model realization and development cases used during candidate selection.
- The state-error comparison is aggregated over two closed loops whose actions differ because the estimator changed; it supports package-level repair, not a pure gain-only or architecture-only estimand.
- No measurement noise, initial-state error, output mismatch, model mismatch, cross-deleted state basis, fresh holdout, nonlinear physical simulator, topology change, communication graph, distributed execution, agent, learning algorithm, EMT, HIL, hardware, or deployment condition was tested.
- Output-energy ratios are squared regulated-output scores, not electrical energy, frequency-restoration evidence, transient-stability evidence, or a safety certificate.
- EVAL is intentionally not run because no learned or distributed policy is under examination; it remains diagnostic-only and cannot replace the registered physical/evidence gates.
