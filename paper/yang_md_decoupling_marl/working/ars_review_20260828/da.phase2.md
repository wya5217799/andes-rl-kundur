## Review Body

The manuscript is unusually disciplined about what its data do not prove: it separates deterministic-fresh from learned-canary evidence, does not pool horizons, and explicitly denies a causal or universal MARL claim. That containment keeps the central counting result coherent.

### Strongest Counter-Argument

An opposing scholar could accept every reported count and still reject the paper's implied scientific lesson. The learned-policy verdict is defined by action-RMS and action-variation limits fixed at 110% of a development-selected direct-M/D comparator. The manuscript does not report the action-stress ratios, their margins above 1.10, or any sensitivity of the 0/208 result to a plausible range of comparator-relative limits. It also states that these limits are not absolute hardware-energy bounds. Therefore, 832/832 establishes only that every tested block lies on one side of one registered cutoff; it does not show whether the violations are negligible, severe, or robust to a different but still defensible allowance.

A stronger rival explanation is controller-class/comparator mismatch. The smooth local-neighbour direct law was selected from a small deterministic family and then became the normalization reference, whereas the frozen SAC policies can achieve lower endpoint energies by moving M/D more aggressively or variably. Under a different action budget, comparator, or cost model, some of the 126 endpoint-qualified policies might remain acceptable. The manuscript correctly avoids calling the co-occurrence causal or universal, but that restraint leaves the central evaluation lesson narrower than its framing: this exact empirical contract rejects the policies; the evidence does not yet establish that endpoint-only assessment is broadly misleading. Reporting action-ratio distributions and margins, testing qualification across prespecified thresholds and comparators, and connecting the chosen limits to an explicit decision risk would answer this counterargument without changing the bounded dataset.
