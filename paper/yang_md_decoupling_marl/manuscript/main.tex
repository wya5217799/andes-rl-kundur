\begin{abstract}
Coordination among paralleled virtual synchronous generators (VSGs) cannot be
inferred from average-frequency recovery alone because aggregate support and
relative motion are different physical coordinates. This paper evaluates a
direct inertia/damping multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) formulation
under a guard-first contract that separates common-frequency restoration,
off-diagonal common--differential response, disturbance-driven differential
energy, and command magnitude/variation. Each of four actors writes only its own bounded,
slew-limited inertia/damping pair. A sequence of matched repairs makes the
 learning trajectories valid and removes selected guard failures, but none of the
 reported valid direct-$M/D$ configurations satisfies the complete physical
 contract. We then estimate a dataset-level algorithmic source contrast with a
 prospectively frozen eight-arm factorial,
six training seeds, and a same-time row-permuted placebo. Authentic neighbour
sources yield a signed geometric effect of $-2.45\%$ for the actor factor
(unfavourable to the authentic source) and $+4.52\%$ for the critic factor;
exact sign-flip tests with Holm control
did not establish either effect above the $10\%$ materiality bar. This is a bounded
``not established above $10\%$'' result, not evidence of zero communication
 value. A distinct structured energy-port controller passes its own registered
 development and unseen-bank contract. This related, non-pooled witness keeps
 the negative MARL conclusion specific to the tested action, learner, and
 $43{,}200$-step schedule.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Virtual synchronous generators reproduce swing-like behaviour in converter
controls and expose configurable inertia and damping~\cite{zhong2011synchronverters,darco2013vsm}.
When several VSGs operate in
parallel, however, their average frequency and their relative motion are not
the same control objective. Consensus and mutual-damping controllers address
frequency restoration and oscillatory interaction with explicit information
structures~\cite{liu2020consensus,shi2021restoration,fu2022mutual,gao2024mutual,
wang2024consensus}.
The distinction is visible in a localized disturbance: a fleet-wide inertia
change directly addresses aggregate support but not inter-unit motion, whereas
spatially different commands target relative motion and may create additional
command variation. Figure~\ref{fig:contract} makes these two readouts explicit.

Learning-based VSG controllers adapt virtual parameters without requiring an
explicit plant model~\cite{yang2023ddic,lu2024sac,benhmidouch2024rlvsg,
zhang2024maddpg,kang2025madrl,li2026adaptive,ikram2026madrlffr,
ding2024review}.
Here, we ask a different question from success-oriented controller design:
whether neighbour-source access adds differential coordination after actor,
critic, reward, execution semantics, and physical endpoints are matched.
Three evaluation problems remain when such controllers are interpreted as
coordination mechanisms. First, a scalar reward or an average-frequency metric
can mix common support with differential motion. Second, a favourable endpoint
can be obtained on commands that violate amplitude, slew, or command-variation
requirements. Third, a nominal message/no-message comparison can change actor,
critic, reward, or source-distribution access at the same time. Seed variation
adds further uncertainty to small-run reinforcement-learning
studies~\cite{agarwal2021statistical}. These issues do not imply that
MARL is unsuitable; they mean that its incremental coordination value must be
defined before it is measured.

Our goal is therefore to evaluate whether distributed direct-$M/D$ MARL adds
physically valid differential coordination beyond fixed common support and a
strong deterministic comparator. We define decoupling through two finite-window
physical endpoints: off-diagonal response between arithmetic common and
differential coordinates, and differential-frequency energy across signed
common, differential, and localized probes. Endpoint improvement is interpreted only after common-frequency,
peak, RoCoF, action, slew, bound, and execution-validity guards pass. The paper
thus studies a new evaluation setting rather than presuming a successful learned
controller.

This setting creates three technical challenges. The physical coordinates and
guards must remain consistent across deterministic and learned controllers.
Actor commands must be compared after their executed-action semantics are made
valid, because raw neural outputs and slew-projected plant inputs are different
objects. Finally, neighbour information must be tested against a placebo that
preserves time and marginal structure; otherwise a donor distribution can be
mistaken for semantic message value.

We address these challenges with a guard-first evaluation pipeline. A modified
Kundur system in ANDES~\cite{cui2021andes} provides signed common/differential
probes and localized disturbances. Direct-$M/D$ learning variants share bounded
per-VSG actions, fixed budgets, final-checkpoint evaluation, and a strong local
neighbour comparator. Representative interventions then test action projection,
value scaling, and action penalties without concealing failed runs. The final
source experiment crosses actor source, critic source, and reward access, using
authentic same-time neighbour rows or a prospectively frozen row permutation. A
 separate energy-port controller is retained only as a non-pooled witness for
 its distinct action object and endpoint contract.

This work makes three contributions. First, we formulate a physical coordination
contract that separates common support, differential response, and empirical
command guards (Section~II). Second, we provide a matched evaluation showing that
successive direct-$M/D$ learner repairs move, but do not close, the residual
guard gap (Sections~III--IV). Third, a six-seed source factorial does not establish
an actor or critic effect above a prospectively frozen $10\%$ materiality bar, while a
distinct structured controller bounds the negative conclusion to the tested
formulation (Sections~IV--V).

\section{Physical Coordination Contract}

\subsection{Control object and coordinates}

The model contains four swing-equation VSG proxies. Their rotor-angle and
frequency states satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:swing}
 \dot{\delta}_i=2\pi f_n(\omega_i-1),\qquad
 M_i\dot{\omega}_i=\tau_{m,i}-\tau_{e,i}-D_i(\omega_i-1),
\end{equation}
where $f_n=\SI{60}{Hz}$, $M_i=2H_i$ is the ANDES startup-time parameter in
seconds, and $D_i$ is its per-unit damping coefficient. Actor $i$ independently
produces $a_i=[a_i^M,a_i^D]^\top\in[-1,1]^2$ every \SI{0.2}{s}. Each component
is limited to a change of $0.25$ per update. Equation~\eqref{eq:action_map}
decodes the executed action into local virtual inertia and damping:
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:action_map}
 \begin{aligned}
 \Delta q(a)&=\begin{cases}600a,&a\ge0,\\200a,&a<0,\end{cases}\\
 M_i[k]&=\max\{20,M_i^0+\Delta q(a_i^M[k])\},\\
 D_i[k]&=\max\{10,D_i^0+\Delta q(a_i^D[k])\}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The learner bank spans $M_i^0\in[140,260]$~s and
$D_i^0\in[50,150]$ on the same per-unit model base. No actor directly injects
active power.

Let $\Delta f\in\mathbb{R}^4$ denote the physical \SI{60}{Hz} frequency
deviations. We use the arithmetic common coordinate $z_c=\mathbf{1}^\top
\Delta f/4$. Equation~\eqref{eq:coordinates} defines the differential
coordinate:
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:coordinates}
 z_d=T_d\Delta f,\quad
 T_d=\begin{bmatrix}
 1/2&1/2&-1/2&-1/2\\
 1/\sqrt2&-1/\sqrt2&0&0\\
 0&0&1/\sqrt2&-1/\sqrt2
 \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
These are registered arithmetic coordinates, not identified eigenmodes. For
signed probe kind $k\in\{c,d,\ell\}$, define the odd response
$\widetilde f_k[n]=(\Delta f_k^+[n]-\Delta f_k^-[n])/2$,
$z_{c,k}[n]=\mathbf1^\top\widetilde f_k[n]/4$, and
$z_{d,k}[n]=T_d\widetilde f_k[n]$. With signed-pair magnitude
$\varepsilon_k$, the two lower-is-better endpoints are
\begin{align}
 J_{\mathrm{cross}}={}&\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon_c^2}
 \sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\|z_{d,c}[n]\|_2^2}{3}
 +\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon_d^2}
 \sum_{n=1}^{N}z_{c,d}^2[n], \label{eq:jcross}\\
 J_d={}&\sum_{k\in\{c,d,\ell\}}
 \frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon_k^2}
 \sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\|z_{d,k}[n]\|_2^2}{3}, \label{eq:jd}
\end{align}
where $N=30$ and $\Delta t=\SI{0.2}{s}$. The positive input vectors are
$u_c=\varepsilon_c[1,1,1,1]^\top/4$,
$u_d=\varepsilon_d[1,1,-1,-1]^\top/4$, and
$u_\ell=\varepsilon_\ell e_j$; each has a matched negative pair. The frozen
profile table supplies every $\varepsilon_k$ and localized-load index. Both
endpoints have units $\mathrm{Hz^2\,s/pu^2}$ and are discrete $L_2$ response
energies, not joules, storage functions, or Lyapunov functions. Thus $J_d$
includes the common, differential, and localized pairs. The arithmetic $z_c$
is not an inertia-weighted centre of inertia, and $T_d$ is not a modal basis.

\subsection{Guard-first decision}

A controller is interpreted only after its trajectories complete and its
identity, bounds, slew, saturation, and independent per-VSG action checks pass.
Common-frequency integral, worst-unit peak, and RoCoF must remain within
$103\%$ of the deterministic reference, while executed-action RMS and total
variation must remain within $110\%$. These empirical guards apply to the
tested bank; they are not stability or safety certificates. The margins were
frozen before confirmatory analysis and encode a tighter non-degradation
tolerance for grid-response quantities than for command summaries. Training
reward is never used
to override a failed physical guard. Safe and constrained reinforcement
learning similarly distinguishes an optimization penalty from verified
closed-loop constraints~\cite{tabas2022safe,shuai2025safe}.

RoCoF is the worst absolute one-step finite difference of physical VSG
frequency, including the initial-to-first-sample interval, divided by
$\Delta t$. Command RMS is taken over all eight executed normalized components;
total variation sums the per-step mean absolute component change from zero
within each signed record.

Let $v=[\Delta M_1,\Delta D_1,\ldots,\Delta M_4,\Delta D_4]^\top$ denote the
decoded direct-$M/D$ command vector. At the synchronous power-balanced
equilibrium, a folded index-1 DAE
linearization gives the channel in~\eqref{eq:dae_authority}:
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:dae_authority}
 B_{v,r}=f_v-f_yg_y^{-1}g_v=0
\end{equation}
for all eight direct-$M/D$ command columns. At this equilibrium, the torque
residual and $\omega_i-1$ vanish in~\eqref{eq:swing}, while the network algebraic
equations contain no direct $M/D$ column; hence $f_v=g_v=0$ and the folded
channel is zero. This is an equilibrium first-order
authority result: it does not remove the off-equilibrium multiplicative effect
of $M$ and $D$, and it is not treated as the sole cause of learning failure.

\section{MARL and Confirmatory Protocol}

\subsection{Plant, comparator, and learners}

All direct-$M/D$ trajectories use ANDES 2.0.0 on a modified Kundur two-area system at
\SI{60}{Hz}, with \SI{0.2}{s} controller updates over a \SI{6}{s}
post-disturbance window. Deterministic comparator selection uses two development
and four fixed evaluation profiles; learner training and confirmatory evaluation
use a separate bank of four development and four fixed evaluation profiles.
Both banks vary loading, VSG parameter mismatch, disturbance location, sign,
magnitude, and spatial distribution before execution. A bank of nine local-neighbour dynamic $M/D$
laws is selected on development data. The selected law reduces off-diagonal
and disturbance differential energy by $60.79\%$ and $64.13\%$, respectively,
relative to zero action while passing every development guard. It is therefore
a stronger comparator than zero action.

The learning studies include parameter-shared multi-agent TD3 and adapted SAC
families. TD3 uses delayed policy updates and twin critics~\cite{fujimoto2018td3};
the multi-agent critic follows centralized-training,
decentralized-execution practice~\cite{lowe2017maddpg}. Each actor receives its
local active-power, frequency, and RoCoF features plus two ring-neighbour
frequency/RoCoF pairs. No-message rows retain the same dimension but mask the
four neighbour entries in every actor path. Training uses $43{,}200$ physical
interaction steps per seed and deterministic final checkpoints. Executed
actions, rather than raw network outputs, are used by the physical evaluation.
Neighbour rows are sampled synchronously at each decision instant; delay,
packet loss, and quantization are not modelled. The critic-source factor affects
centralized training only; evaluation deploys the deterministic actor with its
frozen actor-source mapping and no critic access.

The confirmatory factorial freezes the projected adapted-SAC learner. Its
Gaussian--tanh actor and twin-Q critic each use four 128-unit hidden layers,
learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, $\gamma=0.99$, $\tau=0.005$, a $10{,}000$-sample
replay buffer, and batch size 256. Automatic entropy temperature is bounded to
$[0.005,5]$; one update follows each environment step after buffer warm-up, and
evaluation uses the deterministic actor mean. The reward is the unchanged
weighted sum $r_i=100r_{f,i}+50r_{\mathrm{abs},i}+0.0056r_M+0.0056r_D$.
Here $r_{f,i}$ penalizes squared disagreement among the local frequency and two
ring neighbours, $r_{\mathrm{abs},i}$ penalizes squared local frequency deviation
after conversion to physical hertz, and $r_M,r_D$ penalize the squared fleet-mean
decoded pre-clamp command increments normalized by 600. The reward-access
factor switches only the neighbour terms in $r_{f,i}$.

\subsection{Clean neighbour-source factorial}

The confirmatory experiment crosses actor source $\{N,P\}$, critic source
$\{N,P\}$, and reward access $\{0,1\}$, producing eight arms for seeds
401--406. $N$ is the authentic same-time ring-neighbour source. $P$ retains
each recipient's local columns but replaces neighbour columns with the
authentic row of the next actor, $\rho(i)=(i+1)\bmod4$. This placebo preserves
time and uses no exogenous donor bank. More precisely, it leaves the three
recipient-local columns unchanged and row-permutes only the four neighbour
columns. The permutation is fixed-point-free: for every neighbour column, its
four-value multiset and the multiset of ordered neighbour tuples are identical
under $N$ and $P$, while no $P$ tuple is the authentic neighbour tuple of its
recipient. Reward access independently switches the neighbour terms in the
coordination reward; the reported source effects marginalize over this factor.

All $48$ training cells completed
$43{,}200$ steps. Sixteen cells were hash-identical carryovers accepted only
after per-cell identity and completeness checks; the other $32$ were trained
afresh. Each carryover had to match the arm, seed, factor assignment, complete
$43{,}200$-step manifest, reward hash, and base-state hash. Half and final
checkpoints produced $16$ complete evaluation jobs, and all $48$ final cells
were valid.
The manifest-bound reproducibility supplement provides the frozen input banks,
all stage--arm--seed--profile endpoints, interaction-sensitive contrasts,
carryover provenance, curve-stability rows, topology variants, and the complete
confirmatory run flow.

Let $L$ be disturbance differential energy on one evaluation profile. For
each seed, the actor-source effect is the equal-weight mean of
$\log[L(P,c,r)/L(N,c,r)]$ over matched critic sources, reward-access values,
and profiles; the critic-source effect is defined analogously over matched
actor sources. Positive values therefore favour the authentic source. The
primary estimand is the across-seed mean at the final checkpoint. The
materiality threshold is $\log(1.10)$. At this boundary, we assume joint sign
invariance of the six paired seed effects and monotone symmetric location
shifts. We then enumerate all $2^6$ seed-level sign flips, test each factor
one-sided, and apply Holm control across the two tests~\cite{holm1979simple}.
The descriptive $95\%$ intervals are percentile intervals from all $6^6$
ordered seed-level resamples. As an asymmetric-distribution sensitivity, exact
binomial sign tests for a seed-median effect above the same bar count $1/6$
actor and $2/6$ critic effects above it ($p=0.9844$ and $0.8906$); this is a
different estimand from the primary mean. Half/final direction consistency and
leave-one-seed-out means are also reported. The frozen curve-stability check
compares median absolute actor/critic losses in the final two training deciles
and requires an absolute log-ratio no larger than $\log(1.25)$; it does not
establish that the source-effect estimand has plateaued.

\subsection{Direct-$M/D$ learning remains guard limited}

The deterministic development-selected law is also selected by a finite
outcome-seeing oracle on all four evaluation profiles, so the original
nine-law family provides no additional profile-conditional headroom. A later
$350$-schedule time-varying family contains one development-selected schedule
that is guard-clean on two of four fixed evaluation profiles. Thus direct
$M/D$ authority is not empty, but the finite evidence does not supply a
uniform transferable target for the learner.

Among the valid direct-$M/D$ learner stages, projected adapted SAC provides the
largest guard-valid endpoint movement. Its message arm reaches $0.635$ and $0.590$
times the deterministic reference on the disturbance and off-diagonal
endpoints and passes the common-frequency and worst-peak guards in all $20$
seed--profile blocks. RoCoF still fails in $4/20$ blocks, and both command-summary
guards fail in all $20$ blocks.
Adding an RMS penalty eliminates the RMS failures but moves the endpoint ratios
to $1.229$ and $1.221$ and produces $20/20$ common-frequency and $18/20$
worst-peak failures, together with $7/20$ RoCoF failures. The interventions
therefore exchange guard failures rather than producing a guard-clean
direct-$M/D$ learner.

Figure~\ref{fig:tradeoff} gives a non-data-selected time-domain view of this
exchange on the first registered evaluation profile under its common-positive
disturbance. Projection reduces the common-frequency deviation but requires
substantially larger executed actions. The RMS penalty returns action magnitude
near the deterministic trace while the frequency deviation becomes larger than
the comparator. This trace illustrates the mechanism; the complete gate counts
in Table~\ref{tab:evidence} carry the inference.

\subsection{No source effect above the materiality bar is established}

Figure~\ref{fig:source} reports the confirmatory source effects. The actor
factor has final marginal mean log effect $-0.0248$, corresponding to a $-2.45\%$ geometric
effect unfavourable to the authentic source, with descriptive $95\%$ log-scale interval
$[-0.0882,0.0450]$. Only one of six seed effects is positive. The critic factor
has mean log effect $0.0442$, or $+4.52\%$, with log-scale interval
$[-0.0092,0.0908]$ and five positive seed effects. The exact materiality
$p$ values are $0.984375$ and $0.953125$; neither Holm test rejects. No marginal
half/final direction flip is recorded, and all loss rows pass the stated
curve-stability rule. However, actor and critic marginal effects contract from
$-13.23\%$ and $+23.59\%$ at half budget to $-2.45\%$ and $+4.52\%$ at final
budget. Leave-one-seed-out mean log effects remain negative for the actor
($-0.0556$ to $-0.0016$) and positive for the critic ($0.0284$ to $0.0671$).

The marginal directions are not component-wise effects. Conditioning on critic
source changes the final actor contrast from $+14.21\%$ for authentic critic rows
to $-16.68\%$ for placebo critic rows. Conditioning on reward access changes the
critic contrast from $-7.36\%$ to $+17.91\%$. These unregistered interactions are
descriptive and rule out interpreting the marginals as stable isolated actor or
critic benefits.

The supported wording is narrow: within this six-seed learner, bank, and
projector, authentic same-time neighbour sources versus the row-permuted
placebo do not establish an effect above $10\%$ in either factor. The contrast is a
dataset-level algorithmic source contrast: the permutation also changes
recipient-conditioned coupling and source roles, so pure semantic information
value is not identified. The six-seed design is low resolution, and smaller
effects are not excluded.

Sixteen hash-identical carryovers all occupy actor-source-$N$ cells; no
same-round retraining comparison was performed. The reuse audit establishes
file/configuration identity and arithmetic replay, but cannot empirically exclude
a batch-by-actor-source effect. Therefore the signed directions remain
descriptive for the sealed dataset rather than independent repeatability claims.

\subsection{A distinct structured controller bounds the negative result}

The energy-port companion replaces direct parameter modulation with a
state-dependent feasible power-headroom map and a frozen \SI{0.4}{Hz}
ring-edge bandpass. Because its actuator, estimator, bank, and reference differ,
its ratios are not pooled with the MARL results. After development selection,
$K=3.5$ is frozen and attains $r_d=0.938218$ and
$r_{\mathrm{cross}}=0.793730$ on a one-use unseen bank, with every guard
passing. It also passes all ten equilibrium-sound members of a frozen
topology-variant bank: the nominal network, four inter-area line outages, and
five line-reactance scalings. Two VSG-tie outages fail the pre-performance
initialization/equilibrium-information gate and are excluded from performance
scoring.
Across three additional condition/parameter blocks, two pass and a
relaxed-inertia/heavy-damping block misses the differential ceiling with
$r_d=0.9712$ while retaining all guards.

A baseline-anchored residual SAC then preserves the bandpass passes on the ten
sound variants but never improves either endpoint and shows no message
increment. This companion supports two bounded statements: a different
energy-port action object attains its own matched endpoint contract on finite
banks, and adding a learner to that strong structure provides no measured
increment in the tested configuration.

\section{Interpretation and Limitations}

The combined evidence localizes, but does not uniquely identify, the negative
result. On the gauge-fixed, fixed-mode direct-$M/D$ model, the Schur-folded
Jacobian $B_{v,r}=f_v-f_y g_y^{-1}g_v$ is zero for all eight direct-$M/D$
columns at the synchronous power-balanced equilibrium, so no additive
first-order reduced-state channel is present there. Converged local
physical-parameter derivatives establish mixed state--parameter dependence
away from equilibrium; the normalized policy and asymmetric decoder are only
piecewise differentiable at zero, so the local interpretation is one-sided.
The sealed 30-step finite-amplitude ladder is quadratic-leading in all 12
tested blocks but neither separates mixed from pure quadratic terms nor
establishes a global Taylor law. These facts are consistent with, but do not
causally identify, a difficult residual-learning interface.
The repair ladder also shows that critic scale, command execution, command
summaries, and frequency restoration are distinct failure axes: correcting one
does not imply that the others close. The energy-port result suggests that the
choice of physical action basis can matter more than increasing learner freedom,
but the unmatched objects prevent a causal ranking.

The conclusions cover one phasor-domain simulator, one modified Kundur plant,
registered finite banks, the tested learner families, the fixed $43{,}200$-step
schedule, and at most six training seeds. Arithmetic common/differential
coordinates are neither modal eigenvectors nor inertia-weighted COI coordinates;
time-domain guards are not stability or safety certificates. The topology
variants do not support arbitrary graph or size generalization, and no EMT,
HIL, hardware, or field claim is made. Most importantly, failure to establish a
$10\%$ source effect is not an equivalence test and does not imply zero
communication value. Communication is idealized as synchronous and noiseless;
delay, loss, and quantization remain outside the tested contract.

\section{Conclusion}

This paper has evaluated decoupling-oriented MARL coordination under a
guard-first physical contract. With matched action semantics and a clean
row-permuted placebo, the reported direct-$M/D$ configurations have neither
closed the full coordination contract nor established an actor or critic marginal
source effect above $10\%$ within six seeds and $43{,}200$ steps. A separate
energy-port action object attains its own matched contract on bounded banks; it
does not establish direct-$M/D$ attainability. Future work should independently
repeat the source factorial, test physically structured residual actions, and
require held-out networks before topology-general claims.
