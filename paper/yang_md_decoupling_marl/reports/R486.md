## Conclusions

- R486 strengthens the supplemental description of R485 without changing its
  formal `VALID-MIXED` classification: endpoint qualification remains distinct
  from complete-contract qualification, and the command-activity gap is large
  rather than threshold-adjacent.
- The post-completion bounds narrow three source contrasts under the same
  symmetric location model, but they are descriptive precision diagnostics.
  R485's registered source conclusion remains
  `MATERIAL-EFFECT-NOT-ESTABLISHED`; non-rejection remains distinct from zero
  effect or equivalence.
- Exploratory checkpoint/recorded-path diagnostics show that replacing the
  time-varying previous-action input by an acausal full-record mean sharply
  reduces actor raw TV on the tested frozen paths. Constant-anchor and actual
  raw-RMS norms are often comparable, but this does not establish a
  quasi-static dominant source. The tested single input replacement is
  insufficient to approach the direct-M/D command contract.
- The decoder may shape M/D TV asymmetry, but probe 03's
  `CHANNEL_SCALING_PRIMARY_CAUSE_SUPPORTED` label is excluded as overstrong;
  scaling is not registered as the overall primary cause.

## Limits

- Every R486 result is post-hoc reuse of R485 data or checkpoints. It adds no
  independent replication, fresh bank, plant trajectory or outcome-blind
  mechanism intervention.
- Exact-inversion bounds are not a preregistered equivalence analysis and do not
  supersede R485's Holm-controlled registered tests.
- Root-cause reads are bounded to one policy/four profiles or 24 policies on one
  profile. They do not establish full-roster prevalence, training causality,
  closed-loop endpoint preservation, or the effect of retraining.
- Normalized RMS and TV remain comparator-relative command-activity summaries.
  They do not establish actuator energy, wear, fatigue, thermal load, hardware
  harm, absolute safety or deployment feasibility.
- All results remain one-topology finite-benchmark findings and do not establish
  topology generalisation, stability, convergence or optimality.
