## Frozen setup

- One fresh sealed 24-disturbance bank; five matched arms: slow-only, fixed
  3 s, fixed 5 s, common-state handoff, and full handoff.
- Modified Kundur V4 plus independent ESD1 plant; frozen droop--PI BESS layer;
  common \(M\) action \(+0.25\); \(D=0\); \(q=0\); 300 steps at 0.2 s.
- The adaptive arms use only causal prior-step measurements, hysteresis,
  confirmation dwell, and a 1 s taper inside a hard 5 s action envelope.
- Bank, thresholds, actions, endpoints, 10,000 paired-bootstrap resamples,
  decision tree, code, and execution were sealed before all formal traces.

## Conclusions

- C1 [sealed-bank time-domain] The formal classification is
  `NO-HANDOFF-VALUE`: neither state timing nor fixed duration cleared the
  prospectively registered joint gate on this plant and bank.
- C2 [sealed-bank time-domain] The tested common-state supervisor exhibits an
  IAE/secondary-peak trade-off rather than joint post-handoff improvement.
- C3 [sealed-bank time-domain] Adding the registered differential-frequency
  and slow requested--actual gap conditions provides no material incremental
  value over the common-state supervisor.
- C4 [decision] Retain fixed 3 s as the bounded ICEMS benchmark. Do not train a
  learned gate or expand to MPC under Q-0048; do not present the benchmark as
  a general solution of fast--slow coupling.

## Limits

- Covers one modified Kundur topology, one fresh 24-disturbance bank, one
  frozen slow controller, and exactly two deterministic handoff contracts.
- The common frequency is the arithmetic VSG mean, not an inertia-weighted COI.
- Fast support modulates the VSG-proxy inertia parameter; BESS active power is
  supplied by the separate frozen ESD1 slow layer.
- The result does not establish that state-aware or event-triggered handoff is
  generally ineffective, nor that 3 s is optimal.
- Unified GFM-BESS, motor-dominated load, repeated events, topology transfer,
  EMT/HIL, stability certificates, learned gates, MPC, and deployment remain
  untested.
