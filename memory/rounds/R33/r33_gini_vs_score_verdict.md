## How the metrics relate

| Metric                   | What it measures                                                      | Sign of ρ vs score | Verdict                |
|--------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------:|------------------------|
| Gini(per-agent ΔH range) | how *unequally* the 4 agents share the action burden                  | **+0.29 to +0.53** | high → high score (✔)  |
| <p, range> / (\|p\|·\|range\|) | how much the action distribution *aligns with the open-loop mode shape* | -0.93 (r27, N=4)  | high → low score (✘)   |

These are independent: a controller can be highly concentrated (high
Gini) on a bus that **isn't** the dominant mode-shape coordinate. R21
does exactly this (concentrates on ES2, Gini ~0.4, while ES1 is the
top mode-shape coordinate at |W|=77 vs ES2's 43). It scores high
**because** of the concentration, **despite** the misalignment with the
mode shape — which is the more interesting and more publishable
finding than the original "preserves mode shape" framing.

## Why the principal subset shows stronger correlation (ρ +0.53 vs +0.29)

The "all" subset includes degenerate controllers — vanilla SAC variants
that converge to the 0.137 attractor with very flat per-agent action
distributions (low Gini, low score, both near the floor). These add
noise to the correlation rather than supporting it (they're consistent
with both "high Gini → high score" and "low Gini → low score" because
they have low Gini AND low score).

The principal subset removes these and keeps only controllers that span
the score range from 0.10 (no_control) to 0.44 (R21). The narrower
filter reveals a stronger and cleaner signal: ρ +0.53 [+0.26, +0.73].
