  **R139-W1 (cluster density analysis of N=91 fresh-scored ckpts,
  zero ANDES)**. CLM-0260 (R137) flagged "discrete attractor cluster

  R134-R136 chain. R139 quantifies the structure.

  ### Bimodal cluster partition (geo axis, current paper_grade_axes scoring)

  | Region | geo range | Count | Fraction |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **Degenerate cluster** | geo < 0.10 | **36 / 91** | **40%** |
  | Mid region | 0.10 ≤ geo ≤ 0.30 | 17 / 91 | 19% |
  | **LSTM SOTA cluster** | geo > 0.30 | **38 / 91** | **42%** |

  20-bin histogram clearly shows two modes:
  - Degenerate core at geo ≈ 0.04 (densest, includes warm-h_0
    inference / r67_w2a / r70_eval_paper variants)
  - LSTM SOTA core at geo ≈ 0.37 (R67-R75 LSTM family + variants)
  - 19% in mid region — sparse but NOT empty, includes R62 / R65 / R68
    variants that partially train (intermediate attractors)

  ### Per-cluster cum_rf range

  | Cluster | cum_rf range | Width |
  |---|---|---|
  | Degenerate (n=36) | [-0.596, -0.031] | **0.565** (very wide) |
  | LSTM SOTA (n=38) | [-0.111, -0.067] | **0.044** (tight!) |
  | Mid (n=17) | [-0.090, -0.035] | 0.055 |

  **The LSTM SOTA cluster has 13× tighter cum_rf range than the
  degenerate cluster**. The 38 LSTM SOTA ckpts form a **homogeneous
  attractor** — they're all the same policy class within ±5% of cum_rf
  -0.084.

  The degenerate cluster's wide cum_rf range reflects two sub-types:
  - "Good saturated" (cum_rf -0.031, paper-§IV-C SOTA per CLM-0118): r67_w2a, r70_eval_*
  - "Bad failed" (cum_rf -0.596 = 19× worse): training crashes / TDS issues / wrong policy attractor

  ### Implication for paper Sec.IV-D

  This is a **quantitative new finding** the project's CLM-0118 / CLM-0131
  dual-SOTA framework doesn't explicitly state:

  > "Across N=91 cached evaluations of 91-round R57-R75 training sweep,
  > **42% of policies converge to the LSTM SOTA attractor** (geo ≈ 0.37,
  > cum_rf ≈ -0.084, ±5%), **40% to the degenerate-saturation attractor**
  > (geo ≈ 0.04, cum_rf varies widely), and 19% to intermediate
  > attractors. The bimodal distribution suggests the 91-round 'algorithm
  > sweep' is largely a sample of two stable attractors with stochastic
  > assignment — not a smooth design-space exploration."

  This reframes the "91-round plateau" as **attractor selection**, not
  "algorithm class limits". Whether training falls into LSTM SOTA or
  degenerate attractor depends on seed × hyper, but neither attractor
  produces geo > 0.430.

  ### Methodology note

  N=91 from `results/research_loop/eval_v4_baseline/*_summary.json` is
  ckpts the project deemed worth evaluating. May be biased toward
  "interesting" runs (deliberate negative experiments + SOTA candidates).
  Unbiased "all training runs ever started" sample would require
  scanning `results/r??_*/training_log.json` (not all evaluated). The
  40% degenerate fraction is an upper bound estimate on collapse rate
  for "interesting" runs.
