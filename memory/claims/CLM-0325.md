  **Sweet spot λ=0.003**: preserves LS1 axis at baseline level (0.353 ≈
  0.354) while lifting LS2 to 0.475 (>R100's 0.467). At λ=0.005, LS1
  starts dropping (0.334) for marginal LS2 gain (0.477). At λ=0.01
  (R100), LS1 collapses to 0.314, LS2 plateaus. At λ=0.03, both axes
  collapse.

  **Mechanism**: hidden-state-norm regularisation at λ=0.003 lightly
  bounds ||h|| growth, keeping the LSTM in a more linear-tanh regime
  during steady-state but allowing strong responses during transients
  → preserves LS1 (large disturbance) responsiveness while improving
  LS2 (smaller disturbance) smooth control. Above λ=0.005 the actor
  becomes too conservative for LS1's larger inertia perturbation.

  **Paper finding**: dose-response curve is a clean methodology
  result — single-policy SOTA improvement of +4.7% via a single
  hyperparameter (λ_h=0.003), with clear physical interpretation
  (LS1 transient responsiveness vs LS2 steady-state smoothness
  trade-off).

  ### Counter-intuitive ensemble finding

  R170 (single 0.4091) is **strictly better** than R100 (single 0.383)
  on all axes: LS1 0.353 > 0.314, LS2 0.475 > 0.467, geo 0.4091 > 0.383.
  But the 4-way **swap** R100→R170 gives 0.4102 < R154 SOTA 0.4119
  (-0.4%). Adding R170 alongside R100 (5-way) gives 0.4114 (-0.1%).
  Only the 6-way (R100 + R169 + R170 = full hreg dose sweep) gives
  marginal +0.07%.

  **Mechanism**: ensemble lift derives from **complementary weakness**,
  not from each member being strict-best. R100's LS1-weak/LS2-strong
  asymmetry creates more "diversity bonus" with LS1-strong R72_w4
  baseline than R170's balanced profile does. R170's balanced policy
  is closer to the other members → less complementary → smaller
  diversity bonus despite better individual policy.

  This is a clean **ensemble theory paper finding**: ensemble
  member selection should optimize for *complementary asymmetric
  strengths*, not for *strict individual performance*. Counter to
  naive intuition.
