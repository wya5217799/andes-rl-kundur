    + medium-sync; droop k≥5 = low-transient-quality + high-sync.

  ### Why CLM-0186 missed this

  Identical root cause to CLM-0430: the dual-metric data was in the
  summary JSON, but the verdict only cited geo. The reasons CLM-0186
  ran cleanly on geo:
  - The 11-axis ranker rewards action utilization (axes 6-7) which
    a passive droop controller scores poorly on (small spans).
  - The 11-axis ranker rewards transient quality (axes 1-3) which
    RL trains for directly via the reward.
  - The 11-axis ranker does NOT directly measure cumulative
    synchronization deviation (which is what cum_rf is).

  So a controller that optimises for sync at the cost of transient
  spikes (droop with high k) will dominate cum_rf but lose on
  11-axis. CLM-0186 was citing the metric RL was tuned for; once
  audited against the paper's own metric, the conclusion inverts on
  one of two axes.

  ### Corrected paper Sec.IV-D primary contribution 1 narrative

  > "On the project's 11-axis ranker (which weights transient peak
  > damping, settling time, and action-space utilization), our RL
  > SOTA (R201 hreg, geo=0.415) outperforms the best classical
  > droop controller (k=2.0, geo=0.197) by 2.1×. On the paper's own
  > cum_rf metric (cumulative inter-agent frequency-deviation
  > squared), however, no RL controller beats the best classical
  > droop: R201 cum_rf = -0.069, droop k=10 cum_rf = -0.037
  > (~47% improvement for the classical baseline). Neither
  > controller class Pareto-dominates the other. RL trades
  > synchronization tightness for transient quality; droop with
  > k ≥ 5 makes the reverse trade.
  >
  > This dual-metric divergence mirrors our paper Eq.14 reward-
  > ablation finding (Sec.IV-D §contribution-5 / CLM-0430): the
  > paper's cum_rf metric measures a different quality axis than
  > what RL controllers are typically tuned for. We report both
  > metrics for every controller in subsequent comparisons and
  > recommend the same practice for RL-vs-classical baselines in
  > primary-frequency-control literature."

  ### Implication for "训练更好的agent" objective

  If the goal is to dominate cum_rf, **droop k=10 is the
  currently-best controller**, not R201. RL's plateau on the
  cum_rf axis (R72/R201/R239/R174 all cluster at -0.068 to -0.070,
  while droop k≥5 achieves -0.037 to -0.041) suggests an
  unexplored direction: an RL controller trained with cum_rf
  directly as the reward (or a hybrid RL+droop architecture) might
  Pareto-dominate. This is a paper-7th-contribution candidate.
