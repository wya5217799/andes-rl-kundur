  ### Critical droop comparison

  Droop k=10 (cum_rf-BEST classical, cum_rf=-0.025 on LS1):
  - **delta_M = 0** throughout (droop does NOT adjust inertia at all)
  - delta_D ≈ 430 (below the 600 bound; uses ~72% of capacity)

  - delta_M saturates 22% (pushing inertia toward max)
  - delta_D saturates 87.8% (pinned at +600)
  - Achieves cum_rf -0.0419 on LS1 — **1.7× WORSE than droop**

  **R201 uses LARGER actions (constantly bounded) to achieve WORSE
  cum_rf than droop's smaller actions**. Action bound is NOT
  constraining R201 from reaching droop's quality — droop reaches
  it with less action. R201's policy has learned a "max-out"
  strategy that pushes both delta_M and delta_D to their upper
  bounds and stays there.

  ### Why "raise the bounds" doesn't help (CLM-0175 R195 widebound)

  R195 (CLM-0175) trained R72_w4 hyper with widened action bounds
  (dM/dD ∈ [-1200, +1200] = 2× default). Result: REGRESSED to
  geo=0.2151 vs SOTA 0.4139 (-48%). Widening did NOT improve
  performance.

  Combined with R256 finding: **widening the bounds gives the
  "max-out" policy MORE room to over-actuate, making things worse,
  not better**. The constraint isn't the bound; it's the policy
  class converging to a degenerate "always push limit" strategy.

  ### Mechanism interpretation (refined)

  R256 + R195 (CLM-0175) jointly imply:

  1. **RL learns a degenerate "max-out" policy** that pushes
     delta_M, delta_D to upper bounds and holds them there.
  2. This strategy achieves the project's 11-axis ranker peak
     (R201 geo 0.415, transient damping wins) because
     max-inertia + max-damping does damp transients strongly.
  3. BUT it leaves residual sync error (cum_rf -0.069) because
     the policy never **titrates** — it always saturates.
  4. Droop's proportional-control structure FORCES titration
     (action ∝ d_omega, with cap k×|d_omega| ≤ k_max). Droop
     never over-actuates.
  5. RL policy class (per-agent normalized actor with tanh output)
     has no inductive bias toward titration; gradient descent
     finds the "all-in" fixed point.

  ### Real root cause of RL cum_rf plateau

  Not action-bound size, not local-vs-global metric mismatch (CLM-0460),
  not paper Eq.14 decomposition (CLM-0455). The root cause is
  **policy-class inductive bias**: TD3+LSTM with tanh-projected
  per-agent actor lacks the structural bias toward proportional /
  titrated control that classical droop has by construction.

  Empirical evidence: every RL configuration tested (R201 SOTA, R254
  phi_f-only, R246 only-phi_abs — different reward shapes, same
  policy class) converges to the same "max-out" attractor with
  high bound saturation. The reward shape doesn't matter for this;
  the policy class is what's at fault.

  ### Paper Sec.IV-D revised mechanism narrative

  > "The RL-vs-droop cum_rf gap (CLM-0445) is explained by a
  > policy-class inductive bias: TD3+LSTM with per-agent tanh-
  > projected actor converges to a 'max-out' strategy (delta_M +
  > delta_D saturate at upper bound 22-92% of trajectory across
  > all tested reward configs). This strategy achieves project
  > 11-axis ranker peak (transient damping is well-served by
  > max-inertia + max-damping) but leaves residual sync error.
  > Classical droop, by construction, titrates actions
  > proportional to local frequency error and never over-actuates.
  > Widening the bounds (R195 / CLM-0175) does NOT help: the
  > 'max-out' policy uses the extra range to over-actuate further,
  > regressing performance.
  >
  > **A path to a better RL controller**: structural bias toward
  > proportional / titrated control. Candidates:
  > (1) actor architecture with linear-or-soft output (no tanh
  > saturation);
  > (2) auxiliary reward penalty on |action|² (encourages titration);
  > (3) hybrid RL+droop initialization (warm-start RL from droop k=10).
  > These are paper 7th contribution candidates; deferred to future
  > work."

  ### Implication for action-space-form mechanism

  Initial framing speculated that droop's "different action space
  (direct ΔP)" might explain its advantage. The probe data shows
  droop k=10 actually uses **the same action space** (delta_D
  field of the same range), just with smaller magnitudes. The
  action-space-FORM hypothesis is therefore reduced to the
  policy-class-bias hypothesis above (droop's proportional
  structure = built-in titration bias).
