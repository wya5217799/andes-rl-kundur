  ### Counter-intuitive insight — winner is the JITTERIER controller

  Droop k=10 is the **most reactive** controller (mean|dAd|=9.7, max|dAd|=600 —
  hits bound jitter once). Its responsiveness to instantaneous |dω| via
  `a = clip(k×|dω|, 0, 1)` produces sharp transitions whenever frequency
  deviates. **This is its strength**: quick proportional reaction during
  transient (t ≤ 5s).

  RL is the **smoothest** controller class (mean|dAd|=3.5, max|dAd|=68 —
  ~10× lower peak jitter). Why? TD3+LSTM with tanh-projected per-agent
  actor learns gradual policy updates — gradient descent in a smooth
  reward landscape converges to smoothly-varying actor outputs.

  **Together with R256/CLM-0470 (over-actuation finding)**: the RL plateau
  on cum_rf reflects a **smooth-but-loud** policy attractor (high mean,
  low jitter) vs droop k=10's **reactive-and-titrated** strategy (lower
  mean, high jitter, proportional to instantaneous error).

  ### Both LAMBDA_SMOOTH directions empirically DEAD

  Per existing CLM ledger:
  - **LAMBDA_SMOOTH > 0 (smoothness penalty)**: would make RL EVEN smoother
    than its current 0.35× droop ratio. Wrong direction; would WORSEN
    cum_rf gap by pushing RL further from droop's high-jitter regime.
  - **LAMBDA_SMOOTH < 0 (anti-smoothness, reward variation)**: empirically
    catastrophic via **exploration-noise hijack** (CLM-0058 R50: -67% vs
    baseline; CLM-0062 R55: windowed variant identical 0.110 collapse;
    CLM-0142 R81: TD3+LSTM lambda_smooth=-1 → geo=0.010). Mechanism:
    Gaussian exploration noise creates per-step action variation
    regardless of actor output → reward decouples from policy. Hijack-
    resistant variants tested in R55, refuted.

  **Conclusion**: smoothness-axis reward shaping cannot bridge the RL→droop
  cum_rf gap. The cum_rf-improving mechanism must be elsewhere.
