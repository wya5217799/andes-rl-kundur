statement: |
  R264 evaluated the pre-registered common/differential-mode droop-residual
  gate at alpha_cap={0.25, 0.50, 1.00} on canonical LS1+LS2 with real ANDES.
  All six controller traces completed 150/150 steps with explicit legacy
  50-Hz and physical 60-Hz provenance.

  Alpha_cap=0.25 produced geo=0.355574 and cum_rf=-0.055514. Its geo was
  0.005331 above piecewise interpolation of the measured R262 static-blend
  frontier at the same cum_rf, passing the pre-registered 0.005 mechanism
  threshold by 0.000331. It missed the balanced cum_rf threshold by 0.000514
  and did not dominate the R201/droop endpoints. Alpha_cap=0.50 had negative
  frontier lift (-0.032005). Alpha_cap=1.00 improved cum_rf beyond pure droop
  (-0.031758 vs -0.036712) but collapsed geo to 0.126244 and raised per-scenario
  action total variation to 41.57-45.27.

  Relative to static alpha=0.25, the alpha_cap=0.25 gate improved all seven
  non-settling physical frequency endpoints in both scenarios (0.31%-6.20%
  lower) and matched settling time. Yet its geo was lower than static
  alpha=0.25 (0.355574 vs 0.372745). Axis inspection shows one cause: the
  symmetric paper-alignment score assigns a lower score when peak deviation
  moves below the paper target, even though the physical peak improves.
  Therefore geo remains useful as a historical diagnostic but is not a
  monotone overall physical-performance endpoint.

  This is a partial, discovery-set mechanism signal only. LS1/LS2 informed
  the gate, R201 is a legacy recurrent checkpoint, and no held-out disturbance,
  independent training seed, corrected recurrent training, or topology test
  was performed.
