  ### What this means for paper-term decomposition

  R246/R247 together show that the "scalar at s51/s50 needs
  paper Eq.14 terms" finding from CLM-0400/0410 is **not driven by
  phi_h (r_h inertia smoothing)**. The next decomposition test
  candidates are:
  - phi_d alone (R248-candidate, paper r_d damping smoothing)
  - phi_f alone (R249-candidate, paper r_f sync penalty at paper
    weight 100)

  ### Paper Sec.IV-D contribution 5 — updated mechanism nuance

  > "We attempted to decompose which paper Eq.14 term is the load-
  > bearing 'rescue' for scalar at non-s54 seeds (R242 -16% at s51,
  > R246 -28% at s50). Adding back r_h alone (phi_h=0.0056 paper-
  > effective nominal) at s50 produced a controller bit-identical
  > to the only-phi_abs configuration (geo 0.2347 vs 0.2346,
  > Δ<0.1%). The mechanism is therefore NOT r_h-driven; remaining
  > candidates are r_d (damping smoothing) and r_f (synchronization
  > penalty at paper weight 100). Future work."

  ### Connection to hreg-as-minimal-correction recommendation

  R247 result also indirectly supports the hreg-as-drop-in
  recommendation: if no single paper term is the rescue, then the
  scalar's seed-sensitivity is a **distributed property** of the
  paper formulation that hreg's hidden-state regularisation
  apparently captures as a single architectural change. This makes
  hreg-as-minimal-correction even more appealing than per-term
  reward patches.
