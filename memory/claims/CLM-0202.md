  **Key finding — inverted-U peaked at paper magnitude**: SOTA's 6-axis
  is *maximally optimised* at scale=1.0 (paper baseline -2.48/+1.88).
  Both smaller (0.5×) and larger (2.0×) magnitudes drop performance.
  The drop is severe at the extremes: −24% at 0.5×, −53% at 2.0×.
  No TDS divergence at any scale → policy stays inside stable operating
  envelope, but its **6-axis is specifically tuned to paper magnitude**.

  **Mechanism interpretation**: This is **distributional overfit**.
  Training saw only LS1=-2.48 / LS2=+1.88 disturbances at every episode.
  Policy gradient + replay buffer dataset is concentrated at paper
  magnitude → critic value function steep around paper magnitude, flat
  elsewhere → actor near-optimal at paper, drift elsewhere. Independent
  evidence: [[CLM-0163]] surviving "policy is near-memoryless" finding
  fits — a memoryless policy can't generalise across magnitudes it
  didn't see.

  **Implication for the R57-R82 91-round plateau**: 0.391 is **partially
  a magnitude-distribution policy ceiling**, not a pure algorithm-class
  plateau. Training with multi-magnitude disturbance augmentation (per
  episode draw scale ~ U[0.5, 2.0]) could plausibly raise the
  scale=1.0 evaluation **and** broaden the operating envelope, OR could
  flatten the peak and reduce scale=1.0 performance. Either outcome is
  paper-interesting and tests "is the plateau a distributional
  artefact?"

  **R85+ candidate (PROMOTED)**: R113+ = random-magnitude training
  ablation. Single-seed × R72_w4 hyper × 75 ep × delta_u scaled by
  U[0.5, 2.0] sampled per episode. Eval on canonical paper magnitudes
  AND distribution. If scale=1.0 geo ≥ 0.40 → plateau broken by domain
  randomisation; if not → plateau is robust to distribution.
