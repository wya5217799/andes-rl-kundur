statement: |
  R49-α: The R03 obs-augmentation probe (`INCLUDE_OWN_ACTION_OBS=1`,
  appends each agent's previous action `(delta_M_prev, delta_D_prev)`
  to its observation, OBS_DIM 7 → 9) **does NOT improve** TD3 norm
  75ep h=64 baseline. 3-seed sweep (seeds 49/50/51, same as R48-β):
  mean 6-axis = **0.2629** (range [0.2153, 0.2890]) — **−21 %**
  below R48-β baseline (0.334), all three seeds drop below the
  baseline's minimum (0.295).
  Diagnostic decomposition:
  - per-agent dM_span %: 19% (vs R48-β 9–21%) — mixed, no consistent
    increase in temporal action variation
  - per-agent dD_span %: 4.6% (vs R48-β 11–13%) — DROPPED
  - cross-agent corr(dM): +0.35 mean (vs R48-β +0.06 to +0.40);
    cross-agent corr(dD): +0.43 mean (vs R48-β -0.03 to +0.46) —
    coordination went UP substantially
  - dH_util axis: 0.074 (vs baseline 0.040) — slight gain
  - dD_util axis: 0.034 (vs baseline 0.100) — substantial loss
  Mechanism: with action history in obs, the deterministic TD3 actor
  learns a "stick to my previous action" stable point — coordination
  rises because all agents observe similar local history, but each
  agent's action becomes MORE temporally static (self-reinforcing
  loop). The probe added an unintended attractor that worsened the
  temporal-flatness bottleneck rather than fixing it.
  Implication: the V4 actor's static-setpoint behavior is NOT caused
  by missing information about its own action history. The bottleneck
  must lie elsewhere — TD3's deterministic policy is locally optimal
  on a reward landscape that does not value temporal action variation.
  Real levers: (a) reward shaping that explicitly rewards action
  changes over time, (b) stochastic policy (SAC) at h=64 with
  normalized — entropy noise may break the static attractor, but
  CLM-0048 showed SAC underperforms TD3 by ≈ 0.18, (c) recurrent
  policy (LSTM) — major arch change, untested.
