  **Mechanism explanation of [[CLM-0204]] geo crash** (offline post-hoc
  analysis on the same R112 traces, `probes/r112_per_axis_breakdown.py`).
  warm-h_0 geo collapses from 0.391 to 0.017 because the
  `dH_utilization` and `dD_utilization` axes drop to floor (score 0.005
  / 0.006), not because dynamics are worse. **The 11-axis geo penalises
  "saturate-from-start" patterns even when the dynamics outcomes are
  comparable or better than "ramp-then-saturate"**.

  collapse to floor in warm_h0.

  ## Mechanism

  - **Action trajectory**: baseline ramps ‖a‖ 0.16 → 1.21 over ~10 steps,
    then saturates. warm_h0 ‖a‖ = 1.23 at step 0, stays 1.21+ for all
    150 steps. Both eventually converge to the same saturated control.
  - **Range-based axes (dH/dD utilization, agent_min_activity)**: these
    measure the SPAN of action values across the trajectory. baseline's
    ramp produces a wide range (0.16 to 1.21); warm_h0's flat saturation
    produces ~0 range. The 11-axis treats wide range as "using available
    control authority" — but warm_h0 IS using the full action; it just
    doesn't VARY over time.
  - **Smoothness axes (dH/dD smoothness)**: these measure per-step
    derivative. warm_h0 wins decisively (already saturated → tiny per-step
    changes), but smoothness improvement can't compensate for utilization
    floor in the geo mean.
  - **Outcome axes (max_|df|, settling, final_|df|)**: warm_h0 actually
    improves max_|df| (-50%) and settling_s (-20%). The DYNAMICS are
    better with warm_h0; the metric just doesn't reflect that.

  ## Interpretation for paper Section IV-D + metric audit

  1. **dH/dD_utilization + agent_min_activity are RANGE-BASED**, not
     magnitude-based. They reward action trajectories that USE the action
     box's range (ramp / oscillation / dynamic adjustment), not action
     trajectories that simply REACH the box. This is paper-Section-IV.A
     design choice: paper §IV ranks "smooth dynamic policy" higher than
     "step-input saturation", consistent with control-theoretic
     preference for smooth control laws.
  2. **R72_w4 SOTA's "0.391 geo" includes a substantial credit for the
     ramp-up phase** — the per-axis breakdown shows baseline scores 0.20
     and 0.65 on the two utilization axes by traversing the range, not
     by reaching the boundary. warm_h0 reaches the same boundary
     directly and gets 0.006 on both — punished for taking a "shortcut".
  3. **Possible "honest" warm-h_0 implementation**: design h_init MLP
     output so step-0 ‖a‖ ≈ 0.5-0.7 (mid-range), allow LSTM to ramp the
     rest. This is what R121's α-sweep is testing. If α ≈ 0.3 gives ‖a‖
     ≈ 0.4 with comparable smoothness, dH/dD_utilization might recover
     while still getting the cum_rf benefit.
  4. **Paper-honesty observation**: the project's 6-axis geo metric and
     paper §IV-C cum_rf measure DIFFERENT properties. Section IV-A
     should describe what each captures. paper geo cares about
     "trajectory shape", paper cum_rf cares about "frequency error
     integral". A SOTA optimal for one isn't optimal for the other.
