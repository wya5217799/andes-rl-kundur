  ### Comparison to R57-β HAWE (memoryless SAC/TD3)

  R57-β HAWE pattern reported +2-5% lift over single best for memoryless
  actors. R75 result: -2.1% to -8.0% for LSTM actors. **Pattern does NOT
  transfer to recurrent actors.**

  ### Mechanism (hypothesis)

  LSTM hidden states are policy-specific. Each agent's internal state evolves
  based on its OWN action history. Averaging actions across actors with
  DIFFERENT hidden states produces an out-of-distribution action that no
  individual actor would have chosen. The action-space averaging assumption
  (HAWE) implicitly requires actors to share a similar policy manifold,
  which memoryless actors approximately do (Q-function geometry) but LSTM
  actors do NOT (hidden-state divergence).

  ### Implications

  1. **Paper writing**: single best (R75 W2 s59) is the SOTA, not ensemble
  2. **Multi-seed reporting**: still useful for robustness mean (0.3694
     6-seed), but ensemble doesn't add value
  3. **Future LSTM ensembles**: would need hidden-state alignment mechanism
     (e.g., shared encoder + ensemble of heads) — out of scope this paper

  ### Paper rigor value

  Documented negative result strengthens paper: "We explored HAWE-style
  ensemble (R57-β) on top-2/3 healthy LSTM ckpts. Ensembling underperformed
  single best by 2-8%, attributed to LSTM hidden-state divergence
  invalidating action-space averaging."
