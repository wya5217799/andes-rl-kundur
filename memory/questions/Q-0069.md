## Why it matters

R312 validates signed authority, near-linear paired responses, and nonzero
cross blocks on the development bank. A controller or distributed agent still
needs a compact predictor that preserves those measured interactions rather
than assuming exact decoupling. Reusing R312 for both fitting and headline
validation would not test generalization, so new held-out probes must be sealed
before execution.

## Candidates

- Fit an explicit affine or local linear common/differential predictor using
  only the R312 zero and paired-response records, retaining every estimated
  common/differential cross block.
- Before fitting diagnostics are interpreted, freeze new held-out pulse
  amplitudes and operating conditions, prediction endpoints, error norms,
  tolerances, and failure handling.
- Compare the coupling-retaining predictor against an otherwise identical
  block-diagonal ablation to isolate the information value of retained cross
  blocks. Both arms use the same data and estimator budget.
- Stop after predictor PASS, predictor NO-GO, or invalid validation. Do not
  implement a controller, distributed-agent runtime, reward, or neural
  training in this question.
