## Why it matters

R264 used LS1/LS2 both to motivate and to evaluate the mode-ratio gate. Its
low-capacity point produced a small pre-registered frontier lift and improved
the reported physical frequency endpoints relative to static alpha 0.25, but
that is discovery-set evidence. Before training a new residual policy, the
mechanism must survive prospective, paired disturbances that were not used to
choose the gate.

This question is also the first test of the revised evaluation hierarchy:
physical outcomes and normalized differential-mode loss are primary; the
historical `geo` score is not defined as an overall endpoint outside its two
paper-anchor scenarios.

## Pre-commitment for the future round

- Generate a deterministic random disturbance bank with
  `generate_test_scenarios(..., include_anchors=False)`.
- Persist and hash the bank before launching controller evaluations.
- Evaluate exactly R201, droop k=10, static alpha=0.25, and the frozen R264
  gate (`ratio_full_scale=0.05`, `alpha_cap=0.25`) on paired scenarios.
- Do not change the bank, controller parameters, threshold, or capacity after
  observing any test trajectory.
- Report paired effect estimates and bootstrap intervals, failure rate,
  worst-case/CVaR, physical frequency endpoints, normalized synchronisation
  loss, and action variation.
- Treat R201 only as a legacy frozen component; corrected recurrent training
  begins only after this mechanism replication is decided.

## Decision direction

- A consistent paired physical improvement with no tail/failure regression
  advances to corrected multi-seed residual training.
- A null or reversed result closes this exact hand-designed gate and triggers
  a mechanism pivot rather than threshold tuning on the sealed bank.
