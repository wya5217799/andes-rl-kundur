## Why it matters

R265 did not refute the physical state-selection signal: versus static
alpha=0.25, the frozen gate improved VSG-mean IAE by 4.36% and normalized
synchronization loss by 1.86%, with paired 95% intervals below zero. It did
refute the implemented controller mechanism: mean action total variation rose
236.67%, every scenario was worse, and descriptive CVaR90 rose 448.00%.

The next step is therefore not corrected recurrent training and not a
threshold/cap sweep. It is a gate-mechanism pivot: explain which term creates
the variation, then test one prospectively fixed dynamic smoothing mechanism
on genuinely new disturbances.

## Pre-commitment for the future round

1. Treat R264/R265 as development evidence only.
2. Reconstruct per-step `rho`/alpha and decompose gate action variation into
   base-controller variation, alpha switching, and controller-disagreement
   terms. Add per-step gate telemetry if exact reconstruction is insufficient.
3. Before new ANDES trajectories, choose at most one physically interpretable
   dynamic gate (for example fixed low-pass, hysteresis, or slew limiting)
   from that decomposition. Keep R264 `ratio_full_scale=0.05` and
   `alpha_cap=0.25`; do not sweep them on R265.
4. Freeze a new no-anchor scenario bank with a new seed/hash. Do not reuse
   R265 as confirmatory evidence.
5. Compare static alpha=0.25, the raw R264 gate, and the one smooth gate with
   paired physical, failure, tail, action-L1, and action-TV outcomes.

## Decision direction

- Advance to corrected multi-seed residual training only if both physical
  co-primary effects retain the predeclared direction and the smooth gate
  brings action-TV CVaR90 within 25% of static without failure/tail regression.
- If no single mechanism passes, close the hand-designed gate family and
  pivot to a learned bounded residual with explicit smoothness/safety
  constraints; do not try another post-hoc smoothing constant on the sealed
  bank.
