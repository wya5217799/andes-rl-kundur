## Why it matters

R330 passes one fixed estimator-controller package on a protected retained-
model holdout, including five registered linear delivered-output transforms.
That result does not establish that the reduced state, output, disturbance,
action, limit, timing, and score contracts match the nonlinear ANDES model of
record closely enough for closed-loop execution. The physical-simulation
bridge is therefore the next required gate before residual-headroom,
distributed-agent, reward, training, or EVAL work can become eligible.

## Candidates

- First reconcile every reduced-model state/output/action endpoint, unit,
  sign, sample delay, actuator limit, disturbance, and initial condition with
  the current ANDES implementation and official model definitions. Classify
  platform and bridge suitability as `ALLOW`, `QUALIFY`, or `BLOCK` before
  running a closed loop.
- Prospectively freeze a minimal matched ANDES bank and acceptance rules. Keep
  the R329 estimator, controller, information interface, action path, limits,
  and score unchanged; permit no outcome-dependent resynthesis, tuning,
  fallback, or case selection.
- Compare the reduced-model prediction and ANDES achieved response at the same
  declared endpoints. Separate model mismatch, actuator mismatch, numerical
  failure, and control-performance failure rather than collapsing them into
  one score.
- Treat ANDES as a phasor-domain electromechanical model of record, not EMT,
  hardware, or field evidence. Any model class or parameter not faithfully
  represented must lower the claim ceiling or block the bridge.
- Keep distributed runtime, reward design, agent implementation, neural
  training, and EVAL closed until this deterministic physical-simulation gate
  and a later residual-headroom gate both pass.
