## Summary
Written after the R49–R55 six-failure hexagon, this handoff motivates and specifies the LSTM recurrent actor as the highest-confidence remaining structural pivot. The LSTM fix is that `π(obs_t, h_t)` has hidden state encoding the trajectory, so even with identical observations the policy output varies temporally at deterministic eval — bypassing the noise-hijack channel. Architecture decisions are pre-decided (LSTMCell hidden=64, per-agent, critic separate, seq_len=25, burn_in=5, batch=32 sequences, zeros initial state). Full pseudocode for RecurrentActor, RecurrentCritic, td3_lstm.py update loop with burn-in gradient disable (R2D2 hack), and grad-flow verification test provided.

## Key facts (claim candidates)
- LSTM structural fix: `π(obs_t, h_t)` is naturally time-varying at deterministic eval without exploration noise; bypasses noise-hijack channel established in R50/R55
- Architecture pre-decided: LSTMCell(obs_dim, hidden=64) + Linear(64, action_dim) per agent, per-agent (not shared), critic has own LSTM separate from actor, seq_len=25, burn_in=5, batch_size=32 sequences
- Success criteria for R56: primary = 3-seed mean > 0.40; secondary = per-agent dM_span > 30% of paper (even if 6-axis doesn't break); floor = mean ≥ 0.30
- Key risk: gradient instability with RNNs — mitigate with grad clip max_norm=10, lower LR (1e-4 vs 3e-4 baseline)
- Observable: actor(obs, h).grad populated for actor.lstm.weight_ih_l0 — if None, sequence unrolling is broken; verify before full training
