## Summary
H0 sensitivity screen changes the evaluation horizon and blocks cross-H robustness claims pending formal controller checks

## Key facts (claim candidates)
- A six-worker, six-cell development screen compared uniform device-base
  `H0={10,100,300} s` at fixed `D0=100`, zero action, LS1/LS2, and 30 s.
  The record is hash-protected but explicitly non-claim-bearing.
- Relative to `H0=100 s`, the first-6-s frequency peak changed by
  `+92.9%/+89.8%` at `H0=10 s` and `-34.6%/-34.3%` at `H0=300 s`
  for LS1/LS2. Open-loop transient behaviour is materially H-sensitive.
- At `H0=300 s`, both scenarios reached their 30 s peak after 6 s. Even the
  `H0=100 s` LS1 cell entered its endpoint band only at 8.4 s. Treating the
  6 s paper-facing window as a complete dynamic assessment is unsafe.
- Thirty-second endpoints were close across H. The observed sensitivity is
  primarily transient timing and peak shape, not a demonstrated equilibrium
  shift.
- Decision constraint: retain `H0=100 s` and the 6 s metric as the benchmark
  anchor, add a 30 s tail evaluation, and require actual-controller ordering
  checks at all three H levels before any cross-H robustness claim.
- Claim ceiling: this screen does not establish D realism, learned-policy
  robustness, controller ordering, stability, or Yang-baseline reproduction.
  Formal reproduction and evidence closeout remain required.
