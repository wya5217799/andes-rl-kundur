# Extension and disclosure audit

## Prior conference or workshop version

- Identify every overlapping conference paper, abstract, workshop paper,
  preprint, thesis chapter, technical report, or submitted manuscript.
- Compare research question, method, data, experiments, figures, tables, and
  text reuse.
- Create an author-facing differentiation table with `prior work`, `journal
  extension`, and `evidence locator` columns.
- Cite and disclose the prior version in the manuscript and cover letter using
  the venue's current required location and wording.
- Verify permissions for reused or adapted figures and text.
- Confirm that the added material is scientifically substantive and that the
  manuscript does not describe unchanged conference material as a new journal
  contribution.

## Authorship and contributions

- Obtain all authors' approval of names, order, affiliations, corresponding
  author, and final manuscript.
- Record contribution roles in the publisher-required taxonomy or statement.
- Resolve authorship changes before submission when possible and follow the
  journal's formal process thereafter.

## Funding and competing interests

- Match funding agency names and grant identifiers across manuscript,
  acknowledgments, declarations, and submission forms.
- State sponsor roles when required.
- Obtain a declaration from every author, including an explicit no-conflict
  statement when the journal requires one.

## Generative AI and tools

- Inventory tools used for drafting, translation, editing, coding, analysis,
  figures, and literature work.
- Recheck the publisher's current policy and distinguish basic grammar/reference
  tools from uses requiring disclosure.
- Name the tool and purpose when disclosure is required. Keep human authors
  responsible for verification and final content.
- Check confidentiality, intellectual-property, data-protection, and figure
  restrictions before uploading unpublished material to any external service.

## Data, code, and reproducibility

- Match the data-availability statement to what will actually be accessible.
- Verify repository visibility, license, persistent identifier, version or
  commit, environment, run scripts, and privacy or proprietary restrictions.
- Separate available-on-request language from public-release claims.

## Rights and permissions

- Identify third-party figures, maps, tables, questionnaires, standards text,
  and long quotations.
- Record ownership, license, permission, and required credit line.
- Verify that open-access license selection is compatible with funder and
  institutional obligations.
